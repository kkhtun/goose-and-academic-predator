What are the open-access publishing systems?
Open-access publishing systems are models of academic publishing that make research articles freely accessible online without subscription fees, enabling the wider dissemination of scientific knowledge.
Open-access publishing systems are digital publishing frameworks in which scholarly content is made available to readers at no cost, often supported through article processing charges (APCs), institutional funding, or public grants.

How many open-access publishing models and systems exist? What are their specific characteristics in terms of access and costs?
There are several open-access publishing models, including Gold, Green, Hybrid, Diamond, and Bronze Open Access. Gold Open Access provides immediate free access to articles, usually requiring authors to pay article processing charges (APCs). Green Open Access allows authors to self-archive manuscripts in repositories, often after an embargo period. Hybrid Open Access combines subscription-based journals with optional open-access publication for individual articles upon payment of APCs. Diamond Open Access offers free access without fees for readers or authors, while Bronze Open Access provides temporary free access or access without a clear reuse license.
Open-access publishing systems can be categorised into multiple models based on who bears the publication costs and how access is provided. In Gold Open Access, articles are immediately accessible online, and publication costs are commonly covered by APCs paid by authors or institutions. Green Open Access relies on institutional or subject repositories where authors deposit versions of their work, generally without direct publication fees. Hybrid Open Access journals operate through subscriptions but allow authors to make individual articles openly accessible for a fee. Diamond Open Access eliminates costs for both authors and readers through institutional or public funding. Bronze Open Access allows articles to be read freely on publisher websites but often lacks explicit open licenses and may not guarantee permanent accessibility.

How is copyright for scientific publications regulated?
Copyright for scientific publications is generally regulated through agreements between authors and publishers. In traditional publishing models, authors often transfer copyright to the publisher, granting them exclusive rights to distribute and reproduce the work. In open-access publishing, authors may retain copyright and publish under licenses such as Creative Commons, which specify how others can use, share, or adapt the work.
The regulation of copyright in scientific publishing depends on the publication model and the contractual arrangements established during submission. In subscription-based journals, publishers commonly require a copyright transfer agreement, limiting the author’s control over distribution and reuse. Conversely, many open-access journals allow authors to retain ownership of their work while applying standardized licenses, such as Creative Commons licenses, that permit broader dissemination and reuse under defined conditions. Copyright regulations are also influenced by national intellectual property laws and institutional policies regarding scholarly communication.

How can artificial intelligence be used in academic publishing?
Artificial intelligence can be used in academic publishing to support activities such as manuscript screening, plagiarism detection, language editing, peer-review assistance, data analysis, and recommendation systems for journals and reviewers. It can also help researchers improve writing quality and identify relevant literature more efficiently.
In academic publishing, artificial intelligence is increasingly applied to automate and optimize different stages of the publication process. AI tools can assist authors in drafting and revising manuscripts, detecting grammatical issues, summarizing articles, and organizing references. Publishers and editors use AI for plagiarism detection, identification of potential ethical issues, reviewer selection, and content classification. Additionally, AI can enhance discoverability by improving indexing and recommendation systems, although its use also raises concerns related to transparency, bias, authorship, and research integrity.

How does a researcher assess specific needs before starting a research project?
Before starting a research project, a researcher assesses specific needs by identifying the research problem, reviewing existing literature, evaluating available resources, defining objectives, and considering the target audience or stakeholders. This process helps ensure that the study addresses a relevant gap and is feasible within the available time and resources.
Researchers assess specific needs at the beginning of a project through a systematic analysis of the research context. This typically includes reviewing previous studies to identify knowledge gaps, determining methodological and technological requirements, evaluating funding and resource availability, and defining the expected outcomes of the research. Researchers may also consult stakeholders, institutions, or target users to ensure that the project responds to actual academic or societal needs.

What are the reference scientific databases for beginning a research project?
Common reference scientific databases used at the beginning of a research project include Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and Google Scholar. These databases help researchers identify relevant literature, track citations, and explore current developments in a field of study.
Researchers typically begin a research project by consulting major scientific databases that provide access to peer-reviewed literature and citation indexes. Widely recognized databases include Scopus and Web of Science for multidisciplinary research, PubMed for medical and life sciences, and IEEE Xplore for engineering and computer science. Additional resources such as Google Scholar and institutional repositories are often used to broaden the literature search and identify emerging publications.

Why should we be careful which journal we submit our manuscript to?
Researchers should be careful when selecting a journal because the quality, reputation, and credibility of the journal can significantly affect the visibility, impact, and reliability of their work. Submitting to predatory or low-quality journals may damage a researcher’s reputation, reduce citation potential, and compromise the integrity of the publication process.
Choosing the appropriate journal for manuscript submission is essential to ensure academic credibility, ethical publishing, and effective dissemination of research findings. Reputable journals typically provide rigorous peer review, transparent editorial policies, and greater visibility within the scientific community. In contrast, predatory journals may lack proper peer-review standards, prioritize profit over quality, and expose authors to risks such as publication in non-indexed venues, loss of copyright control, or reputational harm. Therefore, researchers must carefully evaluate factors such as indexing, impact, editorial board transparency, publication fees, and the journal’s overall legitimacy before submission.

What are the FAIR principles, and how do they differ from open access?
The FAIR principles are guidelines designed to make research data Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. They focus on improving the management and sharing of scientific data so that it can be efficiently used by both humans and machines. Open access, on the other hand, mainly refers to the free availability of scientific publications online without subscription barriers.
The FAIR principles represent a framework for scientific data management aimed at ensuring that research outputs are Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. These principles emphasize proper metadata, standardized formats, persistent identifiers, and conditions that facilitate long-term reuse of data. While open access focuses primarily on providing unrestricted online access to scholarly publications, FAIR principles extend beyond accessibility by addressing how data should be structured, described, and maintained to maximize usability and reproducibility. Consequently, a resource may be open access without being fully FAIR-compliant, since openness alone does not guarantee interoperability or reusability.

Do you know the difference between APCs and publication fees in publications?
APCs (Article Processing Charges) are fees specifically paid to make an article openly accessible in an open-access or hybrid journal. Publication fees is a broader term that may include APCs as well as other charges related to publication, such as submission fees, page charges, color figure fees, or editing costs.
APCs and publication fees are related but not identical concepts in academic publishing. APCs refer specifically to the costs authors or institutions pay to publish an article under an open-access model, ensuring that the article is freely available to readers. Publication fees, however, represent a wider category of expenses that may apply in both open-access and subscription-based journals. These can include submission fees, page charges, costs for color images, supplementary materials, or language editing services. Therefore, while APCs are a type of publication fee, not all publication fees are APCs.

What is the primary goal of publication (visibility, impact factor, speed, compliance, prestige)?
The primary goal of publication is generally to disseminate research findings to the scientific community, increasing the visibility and impact of the work while contributing to the advancement of knowledge. Depending on the researcher’s priorities, factors such as journal prestige, impact factor, publication speed, and compliance with funding requirements may also play an important role.
The main objective of academic publication is to communicate research results effectively and ensure that scientific knowledge becomes accessible, citable, and useful to the broader academic community. Researchers often balance multiple goals when selecting a publication venue, including maximizing visibility, publishing in high-impact or prestigious journals, achieving rapid dissemination of findings, and complying with institutional or funding agency open-access policies. The relative importance of these factors may vary according to career stage, disciplinary norms, and research objectives.
